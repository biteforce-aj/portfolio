<ProcessStep step="01" title="The brief">

W+Men Leading Change had no website. Their story lived in conversations and social posts, which made it hard for partners and participants to understand what they do. They asked for a website that clearly communicates their goals and tells their story: who they are, what they offer, and why inclusive leadership matters.

The site covers four pages: **Home**, **About**, **Services** and **Partners**. I handled the UI/UX design and the front-end development of the whole website.

</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep step="02" title="Who it's for">

W+Men stands for Women + Men. The site serves everyone the organisation works with: partner organisations, companies booking leadership programmes, and people joining their initiatives. The design had to feel welcoming to all of them, credible to institutions, and approachable to individuals, so it pairs confident headlines with warm, diverse photography and a clear path to get in touch.

</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep step="03" title="Design direction">

The colour palette is built from the client's logo: **deep green**, **magenta** and **warm cream**. Large, confident headlines carry the message, and the photography is real and diverse.

</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep step="04" title="Process and timeline">

The project ran for three months. The direction evolved as the team refined how they wanted to present themselves, so I designed in rounds: each revision tightened the messaging and the visual system, built around the palette in their logo. Designing and building it myself meant each approved change went straight into the live code without a hand-off.

</ProcessStep>

<ProcessStep step="05" title="Key pages and decisions">

- **Mission hero:** a full-bleed photo and one strong statement.
- **Services as clear cards:** Leadership Development Programs, Strategic Consulting and Program Implementation.
- **Partners page:** "Change is never a solo effort" — a photo collage hero, a scrolling partner-logo strip ("In good company") and tabbed partnership stories (SoarHer2026, CELSIR…).
- **Contact band:** a mailing-list call to action beside the founder's portrait.

<ProcessStep step="07" title="What I'd do differently">

I'd set up a clearer collaboration process from day one: a written brief signed off before design starts, a shared feedback channel, and agreed revision rounds. That structure would have shortened the project and made decisions easier for both sides.

</ProcessStep>
